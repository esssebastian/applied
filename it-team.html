The situation

You've heard what AI can do.
Nobody told you who does it.

The technology stopped being the hard part a while ago. The hard part is that inside a company your size, adopting it is nobody's actual job — so it lands on whoever has a free afternoon, and a free afternoon never arrives.

Problem 01

Everyone has an opinion. Nobody owns it.

Someone on your team saw a demo. Your competitor mentioned something at a conference. Your accountant swears by a tool. None of it is a plan, because there's no one whose job is turning it into one.

Problem 02

You tried it. It didn't stick.

A subscription three people used for a fortnight. A pilot that impressed everyone and changed nothing. AI that isn't connected to your data, your process and your people is a party trick — and your team worked that out faster than the vendor did.

Problem 03

The hire doesn't pencil out

Someone who can choose the right tools, build them into your operation and keep them running costs six figures in Vancouver or Toronto, takes months to find, and leaves you exposed the day they hand in their notice.

Questions

The things owners actually ask.

Something not covered here? Email s@appliedinc.ca and you'll get a straight answer, not a brochure.

Does this mean laying people off?

That's almost never how it plays out at your size. What our clients get back is capacity — the ability to take on more work, answer faster, and stop hiring for tasks nobody wanted to do anyway. If headcount reduction is your goal, say so on the first call and we'll tell you honestly whether it's realistic.

We're 18 people. Are we too small for this?

No — that's squarely Essential. Under about 10 people, a monthly department is usually premature, and we'll tell you that and point you to a one-time assessment instead of selling you a retainer you don't need yet.

Who owns our accounts, data and software?

You do. Every account is in your company's name, every system is documented, and anything we build belongs to you. It's in the agreement from day one. A provider who holds your logins hostage isn't a partner, they're a risk.

What if we want to build an internal team later?

That's a healthy outcome and it happens. Because we document as we go, handover is a real process and not a hostage negotiation — and we'll help you write the job description and sit in on the interviews. Plenty of clients keep us on for the roadmap after they hire someone to run the day to day.

How much of our time does this take?

About an hour a week from one person who knows how the work really happens, plus a short training session when something new goes live. Considerably less than onboarding and managing a new employee.

Where does our data go, and is it training someone's model?

Canada, by default, in accounts your company owns. We use business agreements that prohibit your data being used to train anyone's model, and we'll put both commitments in the contract rather than asking you to take our word for it. We're a Canadian company working within Canadian privacy legislation.

Which AI do you use?

Whichever one is right for the job, and we're not on commission from any of them. This field moves every few months — part of what you're paying for is that when something better or cheaper arrives, we move you onto it and you never have to think about it. Being locked to one vendor is how companies end up stuck.

Can you come on-site?

Yes in Metro Vancouver, where we're based. Elsewhere in Canada we work remotely for day-to-day support and arrange on-site visits for the work that genuinely requires hands on hardware.

We've been burned by a technology vendor before.

Most of our clients have been. It's exactly why we bill monthly and cancellable instead of asking for a large project deposit, and why you own everything from the start. We'd rather earn month ten than lock you into it.
